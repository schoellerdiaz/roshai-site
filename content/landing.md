## gap
eyebrow: THE GAP
heading: Capability is no longer the constraint. Discipline is.
body:
AI can now collect, translate and summarize the online record faster than any research team. Speed also multiplies whatever errors a system carries: a misattributed name, a duplicated incident, a confident summary of a source nobody opened.

For a foundation vetting a grantee or a family weighing its exposure, those errors land on real people and real decisions. ROSHAI designs its systems around that risk. Automation handles collection and first-pass synthesis. Analysts set the questions, test the sources and sign every conclusion.

## offerings

### offering
label: FOR FAMILY OFFICES AND PRINCIPALS
title: Principal and Executive Exposure Reviews
body: What a motivated researcher could assemble about a principal and their household from the online record: addresses, relationships, legacy accounts, breach data. Findings come ranked by exposure, with the steps that reduce it.

### offering
label: FOR FOUNDATION TRUSTEES
title: Institutional and Grantee Risk Assessments
body: Open-source diligence on prospective grantees, partners and their principals: governance, funding networks, filings and adverse media. Each finding is graded by the strength of its sources, and each non-finding is stated.

### offering
label: FOR RISK CONSULTANCIES
title: Human-Augmented OSINT Architecture
body: Workflow design for teams adopting AI in open-source research: where automation runs, where analysts review, how sources are logged, and where AI errors are caught before they reach a client.

## principal

bio:
David Schoeller-Diaz has spent two decades turning large volumes of open information into decisions for the people who carry their consequences. He led OSINT investigations into disinformation, illicit finance and transnational threats as program lead on an analytical contract for U.S. Southern Command. In Colombia, he coordinated the teams that systematized about 165,000 civic proposals into briefings for the Office of the High Commissioner for Peace. In Ethiopia, he served as deputy and acting country director for iMMAP.

He lectures at the University of San Francisco on responsible AI deployment, the AI-cyber nexus and AI-enabled open-source intelligence. He holds an MA in Law and Diplomacy from The Fletcher School at Tufts and an Executive Master in International Business from ESCP Business School, and is based in Oakland.
